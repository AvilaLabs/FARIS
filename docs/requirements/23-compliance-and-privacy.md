# Compliance and privacy

What FARIS owes under its licence, its dependencies' licences, its data licences, privacy rules and its own wording rules about what its outputs may claim. Nothing here is legal advice; every item that depends on law is marked for counsel before the stated gate. FARIS outputs are research screening, never a licensing or safety claim, and FARIS is a design model, not a digital twin, until a live plant data path exists. The accessibility audit and its conformance report are owned by the accessibility file; this file only requires that the report is published. See the [index](README.md#reference-hardware-and-models) for the reference hardware and models.

## Traps

- A notices file that covers one platform is not an inventory for all. Compare against every shipped target, not the build machine.
- "Open source" data is not always redistributable. A download page with no licence statement is not permission.
- Permissive dependency licences can still fail AGPL compatibility (for example GPL-2.0-only alone). Check the declared expression, then use the alternative the crate offers.
- A privacy statement that lists what is collected is only true if network traces agree. Verify against traffic, not intent.
- "Anonymous" telemetry from a design tool can reveal an unreleased plant design. Default off and no study content.
- A disclaimer in the footer does not make a result a screening result. The numbers must carry kind labels and fail closed too.
- Wording lints catch phrases, not intent. Keep the list short, versioned and reviewed, and pair it with review of new copy.
- Export-control tooling that decides status will be wrong sometimes. Marking is safe; deciding is not.
