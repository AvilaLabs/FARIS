## 1. Best-in-class landscape

### 1.1 Fission core design and lifecycle suites (the reference standard for "lifecycle physics tool")

What they compute across fuel life (table stakes for any "plant over time" tool):
- Two-step chain: lattice code (CASMO5, Polaris, ARCADIA lattice) generates cross-section/depletion libraries; nodal code (SIMULATE5, ARCADIA core, PARCS) does 3D full-core cycle depletion with thermal-hydraulic and xenon/samarium feedback, then multi-cycle, reload/shuffle search, in-core monitoring, and licensing uncertainty. Studsvik CMS5 packages CASMO5/SIMULATE5/SNF (spent-fuel) as a lifecycle chain; ANS 2026-09 article on CMS5 fuel management: https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-22/article-8322/powering-smarter-nuclear-fuel-management-with-cms5/
- Accuracy numbers (published, validated against plant data/benchmarks):
  - CMS5 (CASMO5/SIMULATE5, ENDF/B-VII.1) BEAVRS: [U] HZP all-rods-out critical boron within 10 ppm cycle 1, 25 ppm cycle 2; HFP average error < 20 ppm; boron cycles 1-3 within +/-25 ppm on average, excursions +/-50-60 ppm mid-cycle (attributed to B-10 depletion in coolant). Source: https://www.epj-conferences.org/articles/epjconf/abs/2021/01/epjconf_physor2020_06022/epjconf_physor2020_06022.html and https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349495906_ANALYSIS_OF_THE_BEAVRS_BENCHMARK_USING_CASMO5SIMULATE5_WITH_JENDL-40_AND_ENDFB-VII1
  - [V] CMS5 licensing-grade statistics (NRC-docketed Studsvik RAI response, https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ml1714/ML17143A099.pdf): critical-boron/core-reactivity benchmark over 3,256 observations from five plants: mean difference +23 pcm, sample standard deviation 93 pcm (reactivity units, boron differences converted with predicted boron worth); data fail a Shapiro-Wilk normality test, so non-parametric 95/95 one-sided tolerance limits are used; the worked normal-theory 'what if' limits are -134 and +180 pcm. Example single points: predicted 1365 vs measured 1372 ppm (-7 ppm). This is the real 'licensing-grade uncertainty' bar: a published N, mean, sigma, 95/95 limits, and non-parametric method.
  - [V] VERA BEAVRS (M&C 2017, https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1615229): cycle 1 boron under-predicted by 33 ppm throughout, cycle 2 by 10 ppm; cycle 1 flux-map radial RMS 1-2%, 3-D RMS 3-5% in most maps; cycle 2 example 2-D RMS 1.38%, 3-D 3.83%. Shows that even the best code has multi-ppm systematic bias on a public benchmark.
  - [V] Nuclear-data keff uncertainty for PWR burnup (Diez et al., NUDUNA, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0834): ~440 pcm at BOC rising to ~510 pcm at EOC (one study, one code; not a general SCALE Sampler number). This replaces the earlier [U] ~620-650 pcm snippet, which was a different TSUNAMI analysis and is dropped as a reference.
  - CASMO5 vs continuous-energy reference: [U] assembly power RMS 0.52%; radial/nodal reaction-rate mean differences about 1.6% / 3.8% (same search source; second-hand snippet).
  - VERA-CS (primary text read, https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1344991): [V] Watts Bar 1 cycle 1 average reactivity bias <5 ppmB over the cycle, cycle-1 average 2-D flux-map rms 1.07%; cycle 2 BOC +3 ppmB, 2-D rms 1.77%; HFP critical boron within 25 ppmB of measurement shown; typical PWR reactivity surveillance acceptance criterion 50 ppmB. [U] cycles 1-15 critical boron -9 +/- 17 ppm; acceptance criteria 1.5% 2-D rms / 1% axial rms; 1.6% radial, 3.4% total (snippet, https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1260079)
  - Runtime spread: VERA (high-fidelity transport + CTF thermal hydraulics) [U] Watts Bar 2 startup: 4,130 steps, 892,837 core-hours, 13.5 days on 2,784 cores (https://info.ornl.gov/sites/publications/files/Pub74182.pdf). Nodal-class tools (SIMULATE5) do a full cycle in minutes on a workstation (UNVERIFIED; I found no published figure). [V] Polaris 6.3 runtime decreased by a factor of 3 to 5 for almost all use cases (https://scale-manual.ornl.gov/6.3.2/Introduction.html); [V] original Polaris paper: lattice 2P VERA benchmark in 3 min vs 13 min for SCALE 6.1 TRITON, on a 2.6 GHz Opteron, input 40 vs 725 lines (https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1185407) (https://www.ornl.gov/file/scale-overview/display ; https://scale-manual.ornl.gov/6.3.2/Introduction.html). [U] Serpent 2 assembly burnup: 10-20 min for 3M histories on a 2.6 GHz Opteron (old), full coupled burnup 6-10 h on clusters (https://www.kns.org/files/int_paper/paper/MC2017_2017_2/P092S02-01ValtavirtaV.pdf).
  - Key lesson: the industry runs a FAST validated reduced-order tier (nodal, minutes) for daily design/optimisation, and a SLOW high-fidelity tier (VERA/Serpent, core-days) for benchmarking the fast tier. FARIS's precomputed-MC plus 1 s history recalculation is exactly this two-tier pattern and should state the fast tier's error against the slow tier explicitly.
- ORNL SCALE 6.3 (https://scale-manual.ornl.gov/6.3.2/Introduction.html , https://scale-manual.ornl.gov/6.3.1/sampler.html , https://scale-manual.ornl.gov/6.3.1/tsunami-ip.html): Polaris lattice, ORIGEN depletion/decay with refreshed reactor libraries, KENO/Shift transport, Sampler (stochastic UQ: perturbs nuclear data, geometry, material inputs, runs sequences N times), TSUNAMI (adjoint sensitivity, similarity indices vs benchmarks, nuclear-data keff uncertainty, [V] 440 pcm (BOC) to 510 pcm (EOC) in a PWR burnup study (NUDUNA), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0834), Fulcrum GUI (6.3 added 3D geometry visualisation with results overlay). SCALE is developed under an NQA-1-style QA program (ASME NQA-1, ~150 requirements, graded approach: https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1221/ML12213A598.pdf); I could not confirm SCALE's regression test count (not found).
- SuperMC/FDS (fusion-adjacent, restricted licence; in prior survey): [V] >2000 benchmark models and experiments (ICSBEP, SINBAD; also IRPhEP per other papers), stated in Song et al., SuperMC 2.3, https://www.kns.org/files/int_paper/paper/RPHA_2015_2/RPHA15SongJ.pdf (a statement of V&V scope, not a pass rate); [V] 119 ICSBEP benchmarks in the criticality validation paper (search-snippet, https://www.osti.gov/biblio/23042679); [U] variance-reduction speed-ups of 292x (inboard TF coil heating) and 91x (global flux map) vs analog on the ITER C-Lite model; coupled transport/depletion/activation/shutdown dose. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306454914004587 , https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0920379616306834 . FDS "digital twin" claims: I found only the software papers, no verifiable digital-twin product spec.
- Licensing-grade means: documented V&V matrix, benchmark suite with bias/uncertainty per quantity, QA program (NQA-1 / 10 CFR 50 App B), configuration control, uncertainty method (statistical/Sampler-style), and a fixed qualified-range statement. Tools are not "licensed"; analysis methods are approved with a stated applicability domain.

## 2. Table stakes vs differentiators (summary)
Table stakes (reject without): reproducible runs with versioned inputs; parametric sweep + DOE + sensitivity ranking; optimisation with constraints; undo/redo and autosave; units everywhere; import/export of standard formats (STEP, CSV, HDF5/VTK, IMAS IDS); published V&V matrix with bias per quantity; QA/configuration control; regression suite run on every release; uncertainty on every output; report generation; scripting API; headless batch mode; cluster/cloud offload; crash recovery; help/docs/tutorials; accessibility baseline; licence clarity.
Differentiators: real-time what-if on a validated fast tier; app/template builder for non-experts; requirement-to-result traceability (SysML v2 link); asset-health/criticality scoring (ISO 55001 style); surrogate/AI emulators with error bounds; multi-user collaboration; PLM/enterprise connectors; adjoint sensitivities (TSUNAMI-style) giving gradients for free.

## 4. Traps
- Quoting critical-boron ppm or power rms as the "bar" for fusion: those compare to measured plant data over decades; fusion has no operating plant to compare, so FARIS accuracy claims can only be code-to-code and benchmark-to-experiment. The fission +/-50 ppm / 2-5% figures are validation of a tool with measured data; do not transplant.
- BEAVRS/VERA accuracy numbers are benchmark-specific and tuned over many years; averages (-9 +/- 17 ppm) hide cycle excursions (+/-50-60 ppm). Report distribution and worst case, not mean.
- "Digital twin" is the most abused term in this space; ISO 23247 implies live data synchronisation. A design simulator is a model, not a twin.
- Runtime comparisons: "minutes" for nodal codes exclude lattice generation and library build; Polaris/Serpent runtimes in sources are from old hardware. FOM beats wall time.
- Speed-up claims for variance reduction (292x) are vs analog on one case and are not transferable.
- Benchmark count (">2000 benchmarks") is not accuracy; coverage of the user's domain matters. Similarity to benchmark is the useful measure.
- Surrogate/response-surface optimisation silently optimising a surrogate artefact is a classic failure (DesignXplorer-style tools).
- Feature-checklist chasing: Ansys/COMSOL breadth is a decades-long investment; parity is meaningless without a stated scope. Prefer depth on the fusion plant lifecycle plus explicit "not evaluated".
- NQA-1 "compliance" cannot be self-declared; claim only a mapping.
- IMAS coverage of blanket/plant physics is thin, so IMAS compliance is plasma-interface only (UNVERIFIED).
- All numbers marked UNVERIFIED and all second-hand snippet numbers should be checked against primary papers before being written into a requirement.
