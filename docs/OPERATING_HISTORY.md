# Operating-history ledger and controls

FARIS operating history is a deterministic, conditional ledger driven by one identified fixed-source transport result. It is not a reactor qualification or a prediction. The calculation keeps opening available and in-process tritium, breeder-only H3 production, D-T burn, imports, in-process inventory, process loss, and radioactive decay in one site balance. Only the blanket H3-production response contributes breeder production. Heating, flux, component exposure, transport uncertainty, and assumptions retain their response identities and units.

**Current corrected-geometry numerical status:** the first-wall radial clearance defect was corrected and four corrected-geometry 1M OpenMC 0.15.3 transport records were independently replayed and geometry-audited. The current `continuous-delayed-release-v2` histories were regenerated from those exact records at 600, 500, and 250 s. Independent Decimal binding, mass/energy ledger checks, continuous delayed-processing phase checks, and the expanded displayed-output/event refinement gates pass for both control allocations and both port allocations at 600→500 and 500→250 s. The largest run uses 3,791,387 of the 6,000,000 integration-segment budget. Detailed run, raw-artifact, history, assumption, and gate hashes are in [`operating-history-primary-refinement-v3.json`](../references/operating-history-primary-refinement-v3.json). This establishes numerical repeatability of the deterministic ledger on fixed sampled-mean inputs; it does not qualify transport statistics, nuclear data provenance, service limits, or physical reactor performance. Earlier geometry-defect records remain superseded as reactor evidence; see [`operating-history-clearance-defect-supersession.json`](../references/operating-history-clearance-defect-supersession.json).

For the current D-T convention, one source neutron corresponds to one fusion reaction. The reaction rate is (P_f/(Q_{DT}e)), tritium burn rate is the reaction rate times the tritium atom mass, and blanket tritium production is the normalized breeder H3 production per source neutron times the source-neutron rate times that same atom mass. The documented constants are (Q_{DT}=17.6\,\mathrm{MeV}), (m_T=3.0160492779\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg/mol}/N_A), and (N_A=6.02214076\times10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}). The mass conversion uses NIST's evaluated isotopic mass; the energy conversion uses the exact SI elementary charge.

Tritium decay uses (\lambda=\ln(2)/t_{1/2}), with (t_{1/2}=12.32\pm0.02\) Julian years from NUBASE2020 Table I, article page 030001-19 (PDF page 20), row ³H. The half-life uncertainty is recorded as scientific input uncertainty; current history calculations use the central value and do not propagate its uncertainty into an enclosure. Available stock with constant production and burn over an interval uses the exact exponential solution. Current histories identify `processing_model=continuous-delayed-release-v2`: breeder production is represented as piecewise-constant birth intervals, with decay during the delay and the recovery/scrap split integrated analytically. Release start/end boundaries and upward restart-inventory crossings are exact event boundaries; adjacent production intervals with equal rates are merged. This removes the prior midpoint-birth placement approximation and restart-at-next-step delay; numerical refinement gates remain in place for the rest of the ledger. Historic histories without this method identity used earlier midpoint-cohort or restart scheduling and are not interchangeable with current-method histories.

The [continuous-processing control record](../references/operating-history-continuous-processing-control.json) binds saved CLI results to independent Python `Decimal` checks of all four source/delayed-release on/off combinations, delay-boundary process inventory, and an analytic off-state restart crossing. Its rates and identities are synthetic software controls with all-zero solver/data/artifact sentinels; it makes no physical transport claim.

The site tritium balance at every recorded state is

```text
available + in_process
= opening_available + opening_in_process
  + cumulative_breeder_production + cumulative_imports
  - cumulative_burn - cumulative_processing_loss - cumulative_decay
```

The residual is checked against a relative numerical tolerance of (10^{-10}), with a one-kilogram floor scale. This is a computational conservation check, not a bound on transport statistics, nuclear data, assumption uncertainty, or physical model discrepancy. Delayed production cannot be spent before its release. Simultaneous transitions are ordered explicitly and replacement resets only the named replaceable component's fluence; site inventories and permanent component limits persist.

Exposure accumulates only while the modeled source operates, from the selected component-average flux at its reference power times power fraction and elapsed operating time. A service limit is a user-authored conditional input with component, response, metric, unit, threshold, and provenance. No unqualified lifetime threshold is inferred. A permanent limit stops operation; a replaceable limit starts only the declared replacement outage and resets that component's own exposure on completion.

A sampled component flux of exactly zero is accepted as a finite tally result, including when its standard error is also zero. It is not interpreted as proof that the physical flux is exactly zero, and it supplies no statistical upper bound. Such a response can still drive the deterministic example ledger, but service-limit conclusions for it are not qualified.

The energy ledger keeps thermal quantities separate from electrical conversion. It uses the D-T reaction energy less primary-neutron energy as charged-particle birth energy, multiplied by an explicitly authored alpha deposition fraction. Neutron heat comes only from the identified whole-model heating response; in this demonstration tally, neutron and photon deposited heating are grouped by the transport score. An explicit `transport_heat_recovery_fraction` selects the part used in the conditional energy ledger. The Rust/API property names say `transport_deposited_heat_w` and `transport_recovered_heat`; older serialized spellings deserialize through explicit aliases. The heat terms are not a coolant or thermal-cycle calculation. A user-authored thermal-to-electric efficiency maps selected thermal power to gross electric power. Auxiliary electric load remains signed in net power during off periods; cumulative gross, auxiliary, and net electricity are recorded separately. Missing thermal/auxiliary assumptions or missing whole-model heating leave those outputs unavailable rather than silently substituting values.

Routine output snapshots are decimated to `snapshot_interval_s`; internal integration still respects `maximum_step_s`, source schedule, imports, releases, service trips, and outage/replacement boundaries. State-changing events are retained as snapshots. Routine processing cohort releases are summarized in the inventory, decay, and process-loss ledgers instead of creating hundreds of thousands of event records. UI interpolation is valid only between adjacent snapshots without an intervening discrete event; values at event boundaries use the event-side state.

## Assumptions and provenance

Transport rate bindings preserve the run's OpenMC solver identity, nuclear-data digest, source artifact hash, and scenario hash. The local FENDL neutron files pass a byte-for-byte copy check against the prior local OpenMC-data directory, but no publisher receipt or published HDF5 checksum was available to authenticate that local acquisition; details and per-file hashes are in [`fendl-neutron-provenance-crosscheck.json`](../references/fendl-neutron-provenance-crosscheck.json). The result therefore identifies the data actually used without claiming independent FENDL package authentication.

The executable file `scenarios/arc-inspired/demo-operating-assumptions.json` is a frozen, authored control scenario. It uses a 5 kg opening available inventory, 0.5 kg reserve, 0.6 kg restart hysteresis, 1-day processing delay, 95% recovery, full-power calendar with 30-day annual maintenance periods, no external imports, a 60-day blanket replacement after an authored 1e26 n/m² component-average energy-integrated fluence threshold, and a permanent stop at the authored 1e25 n/m² magnet threshold. The thermal ledger uses explicitly chosen 100% alpha deposition, 90% whole-model total-particle transport-heating recovery, 40% thermal conversion, and 60/5 MW operating/off auxiliaries. These choices exercise delayed recovery, fuel starvation/restart, outages, signed power, and material service behavior under source-derived rates. They are not calibrated from ARC design measurements or material allowables and must not be read as actual plant performance. They were fixed before inspecting production history outputs.

`scenarios/arc-inspired/demo-event-assumptions.json` is a separate software-path control with the blanket replacement trigger at 1e25 n/m² and the permanent magnet stop at 4e21 n/m². Those authored thresholds were selected to make both state transitions observable in a tractable test run. Their numeric values carry no material or design meaning.

Fuel restart uses separate reserve and restart thresholds to avoid arbitrarily short repeated operation pulses when delayed cohorts release small amounts. After a starvation stop, the source remains off until available inventory reaches the restart level. This is an explicit control rule, not a plant control-system design.

The selected fluence variable is the integral of energy-integrated component-average neutron flux. It is not a fast-neutron fluence, damage metric, dpa, magnet insulation limit, or a material allowable. The numeric thresholds are scenario triggers only. `run_history_sensitivity` supports full reruns over recovery 0.90/0.95/0.99, delay 0.5/1/2 times the authored value, and service-limit 0.5/1/2 times the authored values (27 combinations). It bounds the grid to 64 reruns and stores each point's changed parameters, outcome, final ledger snapshot, event count, and maximum mass residual instead of duplicating every snapshot. These are design-space probes around authored demonstration values, not uncertainty distributions or recommended operating limits.

The published 10-day blanket residence-time and 0.01% processing-inefficiency values in some DEMO/blanket studies are design-study assumptions, not universal measurements and are not inputs adopted here. Literature values do not establish this scenario's inventories, reserve, recoveries, component limits, outage rate, thermal cycle, or net output.

- NIST, [Hydrogen isotopic compositions](https://pml.nist.gov/cgi-bin/Compositions/stand_alone.pl?ele=H), row T (³H): relative atomic mass 3.0160492779(24).
- Published blanket processing example with 10 d residence time and 0.01% inefficiency: [Fusion Engineering and Design (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fusengdes.2015.07.021). These are study-specific assumptions, not FARIS defaults.

## Monte Carlo ensemble of histories

`faris history ensemble --assumptions A.json --rates R.json --output E.json [--samples 200] [--seed N] [--threads T]` (engine: `history_ensemble::run_history_ensemble`, method `faris-history-ensemble/v1`) carries the transport Monte Carlo sampling uncertainty through the operating history. It draws the driving rates from a multivariate normal with the recorded transport means and covariance, runs the deterministic ledger once per draw, and summarises the spread.

**What it covers.** Only the sampling uncertainty of one transport run: the breeder H3 per source neutron, each component-average flux, and the whole-model heating power, with the correlations between them estimated from the run's batch-resolved tallies (`covariance` in the rates file, derived from the record's `response_covariance`). It does not cover nuclear data, geometry or model-form error, the tritium half-life, or any authored assumption (recovery, delay, limits, efficiencies); those stay conditional exactly as in a single history. The Gaussian approximation for the transport means is checked by the rejection rule below, not proven.

**Covariance in driving units.** The covariance is scaled from integrated units: H3 per source neutron divides by the source neutron rate, component flux divides by the component volume, heating is unchanged. A flux standard error in a normalized record also contains the independent volume-estimate error, `(mean * volume_se / volume)^2`. That part is kept out of the matrix (`volume_variance`) and drawn independently; for every rate the matrix diagonal plus the volume term equals the reported standard error squared, and the rates file is refused if it does not.

**Sampling.** Sample `i` uses its own xoshiro256** stream seeded by SplitMix64 from `(seed, i)`; normals come from Box-Muller (Blackman and Vigna 2021; Box and Muller 1958); `x = mu + L z` with `L` from a pivoted Cholesky factor that accepts semi-definite matrices (perfectly correlated rates). Draws are made serially before any history runs, so output is bit-identical for any `--threads`. The default seed is the first 8 bytes of SHA-256 over `faris-history-ensemble/v1`, the transport artifact SHA-256 and the compact JSON of the assumptions; the seed used is recorded. `--samples` is 1 to 2000 (default 200); threads default to available parallelism minus one.

**Fail-closed rules.**

- No covariance in the rates (records made before batch-resolved tallies): status `not_evaluated`, "This transport record has standard errors but no covariance between its results, so correlated sampling is not possible"; next step "Rerun transport with this FARIS version to record batch-resolved results". Independence is never assumed.
- A draw with a negative rate (or a non-positive heating power, or a zero rate whose mean is positive) is rejected and redrawn from the same stream. If rejections exceed 1 % of accepted samples (or a sample cannot be drawn in 1000 attempts) the status is `not_evaluated` with the rejection percentage and the next step "run more histories or use variance reduction"; no summary is produced.
- A cancelled ensemble returns an error; partial ensembles are never recorded. The command exits 2 on errors like the other history commands; a `not_evaluated` ensemble is recorded and exits 0, so check `status`.

**Reading the output.** `nominal` is the history at the mean rates. `samples` lists each draw's rates, terminal status, terminal time (the permanent-trip time if one occurred, else the horizon), replacements per component, first replacement outage start per component (null if none), full-power time, final available and in-process tritium, and cumulative gross, auxiliary and net electricity. In `summary`, each continuous output has its mean and P5/P50/P95 (linear-interpolation quantiles) each with a distribution-free 95 % confidence interval from order statistics of the binomial (a bound is null when too few samples exist); first-replacement times are over the samples that replaced, with `n`. Discrete outputs (terminal status, replacement counts per component) give the fraction of samples per value with a Wilson 95 % interval. `series_bands` gives P5/P50/P95 of each component's fluence, available tritium and net electricity on a common 361-point grid over the horizon, with the number of samples at each point (interpolation is withheld across discrete events and after a stopped run). These intervals describe the finite number of samples and the sampling model; they are not coverage statements about the physical reactor.

`compare_ensembles` pairs sample `i` of one ensemble with sample `i` of another and reports, per output, the difference (B minus A) quantiles and the fractions of pairs with A < B, A = B and A > B with Wilson intervals. The pairing is valid only for ensembles from independent transport runs (their streams are independent); it is refused for equal seeds or the same transport artifact.

**In the desktop app.** After the nominal history is calculated and 300 ms after the last edit, the app runs an ensemble for each arrangement in the background (the selected arrangement first, 200 samples, or 1000 on request), off the interface thread and cancellable. Any edit that changes the history cancels it, and nothing partial is shown. Finished ensembles are cached under their full input identity (rates including covariance, assumptions, samples, seed, method) and an unchanged identity never reruns. The History step shows the median and P5 to P95 beside each nominal value, distribution sentences for discrete outputs, a shaded band on the fluence, tritium and net electricity charts, and, for the comparison, the paired difference and "A has fewer magnet swaps than B in X % of paired samples". Study files store finished ensembles and reuse them only on an exact key match ([STUDY_FILE.md](STUDY_FILE.md)). A record without covariance shows the not-evaluated reason and next step as text.

## Independent controls and status language

`controls/check_history.py` implements separate Decimal arithmetic checks for half-life decay, D-T source-rate conversion, atom mass, process mass closure, and heat/unit conversion. Its `--normalized-run` option checks successful run, scenario/artifact/solver/data identity, exact response ID and unit, and the history's binding of the whole-model integrated heating tally and uncertainty. Energy checks are ledger arithmetic; they do not assert physical conservation across incompletely tallied nuclear reaction energies. The script does not validate Monte Carlo covariance. A result can be `HorizonCompleted` while remaining scientifically conditional; that status says only that the requested numerical horizon was integrated. It is not a `PASS` for engineering viability. Comparisons rerun each variant under identical explicit assumptions and report point differences; correlated transport standard errors are not treated as independent, and no inferential significance is claimed without covariance. `history compare-runs` also writes `<output>.provenance.json`, binding the comparison hash to exact run-record and raw-artifact hashes, sampler plans, and input identities. Shared seeds do not supply an estimated covariance.
