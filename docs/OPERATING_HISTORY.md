# Operating-history ledger and controls

FARIS operating history is a deterministic, conditional ledger driven by one identified fixed-source transport result. It is not a reactor qualification or a prediction. The calculation keeps opening available and in-process tritium, breeder-only H3 production, D-T burn, imports, in-process inventory, process loss, and radioactive decay in one site balance. Only the blanket H3-production response contributes breeder production. Heating, flux, component exposure, transport uncertainty, and assumptions retain their response identities and units.

For the current D-T convention, one source neutron corresponds to one fusion reaction. The reaction rate is (P_f/(Q_{DT}e)), tritium burn rate is the reaction rate times the tritium atom mass, and blanket tritium production is the normalized breeder H3 production per source neutron times the source-neutron rate times that same atom mass. The documented constants are (Q_{DT}=17.6\,\mathrm{MeV}), (m_T=3.0160492779\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg/mol}/N_A), and (N_A=6.02214076\times10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}). The mass conversion uses NIST's evaluated isotopic mass; the energy conversion uses the exact SI elementary charge.

Tritium decay uses (\lambda=\ln(2)/t_{1/2}), with (t_{1/2}=12.32\pm0.02\) Julian years from NUBASE2020 Table I, article page 030001-19 (PDF page 20), row ³H. The half-life uncertainty is recorded as scientific input uncertainty; current history calculations use the central value and do not propagate its uncertainty into an enclosure. Available stock with constant production and burn over an interval uses the exact exponential solution. Delayed processing cohorts use an interval-midpoint birth approximation, exact mean within-interval decay survival, and a step no larger than one twenty-fourth of the declared delay. That cohort time-placement approximation is bounded by half the integration step; step-refinement comparisons remain necessary for an application.

The site tritium balance at every recorded state is

```text
available + in_process
= opening_available + opening_in_process
  + cumulative_breeder_production + cumulative_imports
  - cumulative_burn - cumulative_processing_loss - cumulative_decay
```

The residual is checked against a relative numerical tolerance of (10^{-10}), with a one-kilogram floor scale. This is a computational conservation check, not a bound on transport statistics, nuclear data, assumption uncertainty, or physical model discrepancy. Delayed production cannot be spent before its release. Simultaneous transitions are ordered explicitly and replacement resets only the named replaceable component's fluence; site inventories and permanent component limits persist.

Exposure accumulates only while the modeled source operates, from the selected component-average flux at its reference power times power fraction and elapsed operating time. A service limit is a user-authored conditional input with component, response, metric, unit, threshold, and provenance. No unqualified lifetime threshold is inferred. A permanent limit stops operation; a replaceable limit starts only the declared replacement outage and resets that component's own exposure on completion.

A sampled component flux of exactly zero is accepted as a finite tally result, including when its standard error is also zero. It is not interpreted as proof that the physical flux is exactly zero, and it supplies no statistical upper bound. Such a response can still drive the deterministic example ledger, but service-limit conclusions for it are not qualified.

The energy ledger keeps thermal quantities separate from electrical conversion. It uses the D-T reaction energy less primary-neutron energy as charged-particle birth energy, multiplied by an explicitly authored alpha deposition fraction. Neutron heat comes only from the identified whole-model heating response; in this demonstration tally, neutron and photon deposited heating are grouped by the transport score. An explicit `transport_heat_recovery_fraction` selects the part used in the conditional energy ledger. The Rust/API property names say `transport_deposited_heat_w` and `transport_recovered_heat`; older serialized spellings deserialize through explicit aliases. The heat terms are not a coolant or thermal-cycle calculation. A user-authored thermal-to-electric efficiency maps selected thermal power to gross electric power. Auxiliary electric load remains signed in net power during off periods; cumulative gross, auxiliary, and net electricity are recorded separately. Missing thermal/auxiliary assumptions or missing whole-model heating leave those outputs unavailable rather than silently substituting values.

Routine output snapshots are decimated to `snapshot_interval_s`; internal integration still respects `maximum_step_s`, source schedule, imports, releases, service trips, and outage/replacement boundaries. State-changing events are retained as snapshots. Routine processing cohort releases are summarized in the inventory, decay, and process-loss ledgers instead of creating hundreds of thousands of event records. UI interpolation is valid only between adjacent snapshots without an intervening discrete event; values at event boundaries use the event-side state.

## Assumptions and provenance

Transport rate bindings preserve the run's OpenMC solver identity, nuclear-data digest, source artifact hash, and scenario hash. The local FENDL neutron files pass a byte-for-byte copy check against the prior local OpenMC-data directory, but no publisher receipt or published HDF5 checksum was available to authenticate that local acquisition; details and per-file hashes are in [`fendl-neutron-provenance-crosscheck.json`](../references/fendl-neutron-provenance-crosscheck.json). The result therefore identifies the data actually used without claiming independent FENDL package authentication.

The executable file `scenarios/arc-inspired/demo-operating-assumptions.json` is a frozen, authored control scenario. It uses a 5 kg opening available inventory, 0.5 kg reserve, 0.6 kg restart hysteresis, 1-day processing delay, 95% recovery, full-power calendar with 30-day annual maintenance periods, no external imports, a 60-day blanket replacement after an authored 1e26 n/m² component-average energy-integrated fluence threshold, and a permanent stop at the authored 1e25 n/m² magnet threshold. The thermal ledger uses explicitly chosen 100% alpha deposition, 90% whole-model total-particle transport-heating recovery, 40% thermal conversion, and 60/5 MW operating/off auxiliaries. These choices exercise delayed recovery, fuel starvation/restart, outages, signed power, and material service behavior under source-derived rates. They are not calibrated from ARC design measurements or material allowables and must not be read as actual plant performance. They were fixed before inspecting production history outputs.

`scenarios/arc-inspired/demo-event-assumptions.json` is a separate software-path control with the blanket replacement trigger at 1e25 n/m² and the permanent magnet stop at 4e21 n/m². Those authored thresholds were selected to make both state transitions observable in a tractable test run. Their numeric values carry no material or design meaning.

Fuel restart uses separate reserve and restart thresholds to avoid arbitrarily short repeated operation pulses when delayed cohorts release small amounts. After a starvation stop, the source remains off until available inventory reaches the restart level. This is an explicit control rule, not a plant control-system design.

The selected fluence variable is the integral of energy-integrated component-average neutron flux. It is not a fast-neutron fluence, damage metric, dpa, magnet insulation limit, or a material allowable. The numeric thresholds are scenario triggers only. `run_history_sensitivity` supports full reruns over recovery 0.90/0.95/0.99, delay 0.5/1/2 times the authored value, and service-limit 0.5/1/2 times the authored values (27 combinations). It bounds the grid to 64 reruns and stores each point's changed parameters, outcome, final ledger snapshot, event count, and maximum mass residual instead of duplicating every snapshot. These are design-space probes around authored demonstration values, not uncertainty distributions or recommended operating limits.

The published 10-day blanket residence-time and 0.01% processing-inefficiency values in some DEMO/blanket studies are design-study assumptions, not universal measurements and are not inputs adopted here. Literature values do not establish this scenario's inventories, reserve, recoveries, component limits, outage rate, thermal cycle, or net output.

Sources:

- F.G. Kondev et al., [The NUBASE2020 evaluation of nuclear physics properties](https://www-nds.iaea.org/amdc/ame2020/NUBASE2020.pdf), Table I, article page 030001-19 (PDF page 20), row ³H: 12.32 y with uncertainty 0.02 y.
- NIST, [Hydrogen isotopic compositions](https://pml.nist.gov/cgi-bin/Compositions/stand_alone.pl?ele=H), row T (³H): relative atomic mass 3.0160492779(24).
- Published blanket processing example with 10 d residence time and 0.01% inefficiency: [Fusion Engineering and Design (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fusengdes.2015.07.021). These are study-specific assumptions, not FARIS defaults.
- IAEA, [Tritium Processing and Inventory](https://nucleus.iaea.org/sites/fusionportal/Shared%20Documents/DEMO/2021/5.Humrickhouse.pdf), illustrating sensitivity of fuel-cycle inventory to reserve and processing assumptions.

## Independent controls and status language

`controls/check_history.py` implements separate Decimal arithmetic checks for half-life decay, D-T source-rate conversion, atom mass, process mass closure, and heat/unit conversion. Its `--normalized-run` option checks successful run, scenario/artifact/solver/data identity, exact response ID and unit, and the history's binding of the whole-model integrated heating tally and uncertainty. Energy checks are ledger arithmetic; they do not assert physical conservation across incompletely tallied nuclear reaction energies. The script does not validate Monte Carlo covariance. A result can be `HorizonCompleted` while remaining scientifically conditional; that status says only that the requested numerical horizon was integrated. It is not a `PASS` for engineering viability. Comparisons rerun each variant under identical explicit assumptions and report point differences; correlated transport standard errors are not treated as independent, and no inferential significance is claimed without covariance.

## Recorded numerical control results

`references/operating-history-verification.json` and its paired-comparison and sensitivity records preserve the actual input identities and derived outputs. The two transport runs each used OpenMC 0.15.3, 100 batches of 10,000 particles, and one thread, with seeds 123456789 and 987654321. The reference and breeder-emphasis TBR point estimates were 0.864859 and 0.867501, respectively. Under the frozen baseline history assumptions, the 30-year calendar horizon completed with 0.8161 and 0.8273 full-power years; breeder H3 production was 20.7696 and 21.1195 kg, while D-T burn was 24.0149 and 24.3452 kg. Both histories ended with fuel unavailable and no blanket replacement. Thus these cases illustrate dependence on authored startup stock, imports, and recovery assumptions; they do not establish self-sufficiency. Their paired-history point differences are descriptive because the transport results used different seeds and no covariance estimate is available.

The baseline 27-point sensitivity grid varied recovery (0.90, 0.95, 0.99), processing delay (0.5, 1, 2 times baseline), and the blanket service threshold (0.5, 1, 2 times baseline). All cases reached the requested history horizon. Service-threshold changes had no effect because baseline histories exhausted available fuel before reaching even half the blanket threshold. Full-power time ranged from 0.6558–0.6659 years at 90% recovery, 0.8117–0.8224 years at 95%, and 0.9997–1.0120 years at 99%. This is a conditional sensitivity result, not a probability distribution or uncertainty interval.

The separate event-demonstration assumptions use a 100 kg opening inventory and explicitly numerical service triggers to exercise replacement and permanent-trip paths. Their 1e25 n/m² blanket trigger generated 26 local blanket replacements before the 4e21 n/m² magnet trigger stopped operation in the reference history. These values are software-control thresholds only, not material fluence limits. The event history must not be compared to qualified component lifetimes.

The independent Decimal checker verified the reference history's source-rate production and burn conversions, plus all 11,004 stored mass-balance snapshots. The largest Decimal residual was 8.93e-13 kg versus a 5.14e-9 kg computational tolerance. A half-step rerun (1 h to 0.5 h maximum step) changed cumulative production, burn, full-power time, and fusion energy by at most 1.79e-5 relatively; final stock and decay differed by at most 6.15e-5 kg. Event kinds were unchanged and maximum event-time movement was 459 s, below the coarse 3,600 s step. The predeclared numerical refinement criterion therefore passes for this scenario; it does not bound Monte Carlo, data, or modeling uncertainty.

A separate 30,000-history coupled smoke run binds the identified whole-model heating response through the validated source-run record. The raw transport mean is 535.212 MW with a 1.889861 MW tally standard error. The Decimal audit independently reproduces the response binding and checks recovered whole-model transport/alpha heat, gross conversion, operating/off auxiliary consumption, and signed net energy in the recorded history. Over its 30-year calendar horizon, the authored ledger reports 3,559,974.4648 MWh recovered whole-model transport heat, 771,601.7687 MWh deposited alpha heat, 1,732,630.4934 MWh gross electricity, 1,721,381.9114 MWh auxiliary electricity, and +11,248.5820 MWh net electricity. Mass-balance residual stays below 9.88e-13 kg.

This smoke result does **not** evaluate physical source-to-heating energy closure. The 525 MW D-T source convention partitions about 420.597 MW into primary neutrons and 104.403 MW into charged-particle birth energy; the alpha part is modeled separately with an authored deposition fraction. OpenMC's neutron `heating` score includes energy deposition based on reaction KERMA, including the reaction Q term, while the photon score tracks direct photon deposition. Thus whole-model nuclear heating is not constrained to be below the declared fusion power or the primary-neutron share. A physical closure would require accounting for nuclear reaction Q, transported and escaped particle energy, and deposited energy consistently; this control does not do that. [OpenMC 0.15's heating documentation](https://docs.openmc.org/en/v0.15.0/methods/energy_deposition.html) describes the reaction-energy balance and KERMA basis. The electric ledger values are arithmetic under authored recovery and conversion assumptions, not a qualified plant output or full heat-cycle result. A separate 1,000,000-history coupled run now provides a higher-statistics binding check; neither run qualifies the design.

The 1,000,000-history run used 100 batches of 10,000 particles, seed `80117033`, one OpenMP thread, and OpenMC 0.15.3. Its normalized whole-model heating result is `536.461843 ± 0.292994 MW` (reported standard error), and the integrated blanket H-3 production is `1.6116074e20 ± 2.1831e17 particles/s`. The breeder ratio is therefore about `0.86561 ± 0.00117 H-3/source neutron` for this sampled model. The magnet-region average flux is `9.2964e12 ± 4.6125e12 neutrons/m²/s` (about 49.6% relative standard error), so that sample remains too uncertain to support a component lifetime statement. The source run, normalized history, and Decimal ledger audit are identified in [the 1M history record](../references/operating-history-coupled-reference-1m-fields-v2.json); its exact source run and artifact hashes are recorded there. This is a code-to-code numerical binding and conditional-history exercise, not experimental validation or reactor qualification.

Further checks using that same 1M source run are recorded in [the 20-minute 1M controls report](../references/operating-history-reference-1m-controls-20m-v4.json). The previous 1-hour controls run remains in [its original record](../references/operating-history-reference-1m-controls-v2.json). Under the separate event-demo assumptions, the sampled-mean history records 31 blanket replacements and one fuel-unavailable transition; it does not reach the authored permanent magnet trigger before fuel starvation. This differs from an earlier event fixture run, because the current production source run and point rates are different. Component-flux standard errors are carried with the driving-rate provenance, but not propagated into fluence, event timing, or threshold-crossing probability. For example, the magnet average-flux relative standard error is about 49.6%; absence of a sampled-mean trip is not evidence that the true exposure remains below a physical limit.

The refreshed 27-point assumption sensitivity grid ranges from 0.658–1.017 full-power years and from about −277,860 to +289,009 MWh of signed net electricity. Both net signs occur across the authored recovery/delay/service-trigger combinations. These are conditional point-mean ledger outputs, not confidence bounds or a claim about likely net output. The 1-hour versus 30-minute integration refinement misses the predeclared absolute decay-change tolerance (`2.291e-4 kg` versus `1e-4 kg`) even though the relative changes in production, burn, full-power time, and fusion energy are below `1e-4`. A finer baseline 20-minute versus 16-minute-40-second comparison passes the same numerical rule, with maximum event-time movement 284 s; the event-demo comparison passes at the same two step sizes once the bounded dynamic-segment ceiling is raised, with 595 s maximum event-time movement; that local refinement does not erase the 1-hour miss or bound Monte Carlo, input, or model uncertainty. The authored baseline and event presets now use the tested 20-minute integration step. The nominal input grid remains capped at one million intervals, while the event-aware runtime has a separately bounded two-million subsegment ceiling for event/cohort splits; snapshot, event, input, and cancellation bounds remain in force. Their regenerated 1M baseline history, 27-case sensitivity, and event walkthrough are identified in [the 20-minute verification record](../references/operating-history-reference-1m-controls-20m-v4.json); original 1-hour evidence and hashes remain intact.

The current event demonstration does not reach the permanent magnet trigger under its sampled-mean driving rates. The permanent-stop code path has a separate [software-only synthetic-rate control](../references/software-permanent-trip-control-result.json), with an explicitly synthetic flux and all-zero solver/data/artifact identity sentinels. It tests deterministic event sequencing only; it is not transport evidence or a physical threshold. This separation keeps a software test from making a noisy sampled magnet flux appear to support a real component stop.
